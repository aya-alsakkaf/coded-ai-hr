**Purpose:** This program helps KOC HR employees give Microsoft Copilot the right instructions, context, data, tone and approved sources, so that its output is reliable enough to use in daily HR work after human review.

## Why This Program Fits KOC

- **Your objective is the backbone.** KOC asked for HR employees to use Copilot "confidently, efficiently and responsibly", with emphasis on the right instructions, tone, data, context and approved knowledge sources. Day 1 teaches these five inputs by name, and every later exercise uses them.
- **Built for the room you will send.** You expect 10 to 15 participants from six HR units, at junior and middle level, with mixed AI familiarity. The program has no prerequisite, opens with a leveling block, runs its exercises in five HR unit tracks, and gives faster participants an extra challenge every day.
- **All ten of your named workflows are practiced.** Job descriptions, job evaluation, manpower, compensation, employee administration, communications, meetings, investigations, reporting and L&D each appear in a hands-on exercise on Days 1 and 2. On Day 3, each participant applies the method to one of them in their own unit.
- **Microsoft Copilot only.** Copilot is KOC's approved AI tool, so every exercise runs in Copilot and the Microsoft 365 apps your employees already use. No other AI tool is used.
- **Recruitment at the depth you asked for.** Recruitment is not a primary focus for KOC, so it appears once, at a high level, on Day 2.
- **Shaped by what Copilot learners tell us.** In our recent Copilot programs, participants asked for less introductory content and more real work examples, and rated building their own assistant as the most useful part. Here, foundations take one morning, and Day 3 is spent building.

**The gap is not access to Copilot. It is knowing what to give it, how to check what comes back, and where its role must stop.**

## Program Outline

Each participant works in one of five HR unit tracks: Job Evaluation and Salary Scaling, Compensation, Manpower, Personnel and Administration, or Learning and Development. Teaching is shared, and practice cases change by track.

### Day 1: Directing Copilot for Reliable HR Work

**Objective:** By the end of the day, participants can write a Copilot request that gives the five inputs, ground it in an approved file, check the result, and save it for reuse.

- **Starting point and setup check.** A short skills check that we repeat on Day 3. Each participant then confirms that Copilot works in Chat, Word, Outlook, Teams, Excel and PowerPoint on their own account.
- **How Copilot produces an answer.** How a language model predicts text, and why it can sound certain while it is wrong. What Copilot can see: only the files, emails and chats the user can already open.
- **Rules before the first request.** Participants sort everyday HR tasks into three groups: do it yourself, Copilot supports, Copilot drafts for review. Grades, pay, ratings, discipline and selection stay with authorized HR staff. Then they practice spotting and removing personal, salary and case details that must never go into a prompt.
- **The five inputs of a reliable request.** Instructions, context, data, tone and approved sources. Participants update an outdated job description by hand for ten minutes, then with a one-line request, then with a structured request, and compare the three results.
- **Grounding and checking.** Attaching an approved policy or template, asking Copilot to show where each point came from, and catching unsupported claims, missing information and confident mistakes.

**Hands-on: one job description, three ways.** Using a synthetic role from their unit track, participants write three requests: one line, structured, and grounded in an approved template and role information. They check each result against the template and save the best request as the first entry in their HR Prompt Bank, a Word document they build all week.

**Extra challenge:** Ask Copilot to list every assumption it made in the job description, then remove each one by adding the missing context.

**Close:** Each participant names one recurring task from their own unit to test on Day 2.

### Day 2: Copilot Across HR Documents, Communication and Reporting

**Objective:** By the end of the day, participants can use Copilot in Word, Outlook, Teams, Excel and PowerPoint to turn approved HR material into reviewed documents, messages, meeting records and summaries.

- **Policies and job evaluation in Word.** Summarizing a long policy, comparing two versions and listing what changed. Organizing job-evaluation evidence against the evaluation factors, without assigning a grade. At a high level: turning an approved job description into vacancy text and structured interview questions.
- **Employee communication in Outlook.** The session opens on a 30-message email thread about a policy change: who decided what? Participants summarize it into decisions and open questions, then draft an announcement and a reply to an employee request, adjusting tone for employees, managers and senior stakeholders.
- **Meetings and investigations in Teams.** Preparing an agenda and background brief, then turning meeting notes into actions, owners and deadlines. Organizing synthetic investigation notes into a timeline and a list of questions, without findings or conclusions.
- **HR trackers in Excel.** Summarizing a manpower, compensation or training tracker by asking questions in plain language, then checking every figure Copilot reports against the sheet itself.
- **Briefings in PowerPoint.** Building a short management briefing from a Word document, then correcting it for accuracy and tone.

**Hands-on: one case, four outputs.** Each participant receives a synthetic case file for their unit track, for example a staffing request for a new section, an allowance policy update, a change to attendance rules, a quarter of training requests or a re-evaluation request. They produce a Word summary, an Outlook message, a meeting brief, and either an Excel summary or a PowerPoint briefing. They review each output for accuracy, confidentiality, neutrality and source alignment before calling it finished.

**Extra challenge:** Write a review request that asks Copilot to audit its own output against the same four checks, and add it to the Prompt Bank.

**Close:** Participants add the day's best requests to their Prompt Bank, organized by task.

### Day 3: Reusable HR Assistants and Applied Capstone

**Objective:** By the end of the day, participants can build and test a reusable Copilot solution for one recurring task in their unit, and show that it works on a case they have not seen before.

- **Reuse before you rebuild.** Finding, adapting and saving prompts in the Copilot Prompt Gallery, and turning the Prompt Bank into a set of tested requests for the participant's unit.
- **Build an HR assistant with Agent Builder.** Writing the assistant's instructions: its role, scope and tone, what it must not answer, and when to refer the person to HR. Adding approved practice files as its knowledge, then testing it with a normal, an incomplete and a sensitive question, and fixing what fails.
- **Capstone briefing.** Each participant receives a new case from their unit track and a short self-check guide.

**Capstone: a tested workflow for a new case.** The case is new to the participant, so it shows what they can do, not what they remember.

- **Core, for everyone:** Complete the case by chaining at least two Copilot apps, using structured requests grounded in the case's approved files, with every output reviewed against the four checks.
- **Extension, for those who finish:** Turn the workflow into a reusable prompt set, or connect it to the HR assistant built in the morning.
- **Peer test:** Each participant hands their prompt set or assistant to a colleague from a different unit, who runs it on a fresh input with no instructions. The owner then fixes what broke.

**Extra challenge:** Extend the HR assistant so that it handles a second scenario and refers out-of-scope questions to HR.

**Close:** Each participant shares, in one minute, one thing that broke in the peer test and how they fixed it. The program ends with the repeat skills check, a short survey and certificates.

## Practical Output

**Each participant leaves with:**

- **An HR Prompt Bank in Word:** tested requests for their unit, built across the three days and organized by task.
- **A four-point review checklist** for accuracy, confidentiality, neutrality and source alignment, plus a request that asks Copilot to run the same checks.
- **Reviewed work on synthetic cases:** a job description from Day 1, and from Day 2 a Word summary, an Outlook message, a meeting brief, and an Excel summary or PowerPoint briefing.
- **An HR assistant prototype** built with Agent Builder and grounded in approved practice files, where KOC's Microsoft 365 setup allows it.
- **A capstone workflow** for one recurring task in their unit, tested by a colleague on a new input.

**KOC receives:**

- **The participants' capstone workflows**, grouped by HR unit track, for HR leadership to review for wider use.
- **Group results** from the starting and repeat skills checks.

## Final Outcomes

By the end of the program, participants should be able to:

1. Explain how Copilot produces an answer, what information it can access, and why its output needs checking.
2. Decide which HR tasks Copilot can support, and which decisions stay with authorized HR staff.
3. Write structured requests that give Copilot clear instructions, context, data, tone and approved sources.
4. Ground Copilot's output in approved policies and templates, and check the output against them.
5. Keep personal, salary and case details out of prompts.
6. Use Copilot in Word, Outlook, Teams, Excel and PowerPoint for recurring HR documents, communication, meetings and summaries.
7. Verify the figures and claims in Copilot's output before using it.
8. Build, test and improve a reusable prompt set or simple HR assistant for one recurring task in their unit.

## Scope and Responsible Use

- **Copilot drafts; people decide.** Copilot may organize job-evaluation evidence, compensation information or investigation notes. It does not set grades, pay, ratings, disciplinary outcomes or candidate selection. Every employee-related output stays subject to authorized HR review.
- **We teach Copilot, not KOC policy.** KOC's own policies, templates and grading structures remain the reference. Practice uses synthetic versions of these.
- **Synthetic data only.** Exercises use synthetic or anonymized information. Real employee data is used only with KOC's formal approval and within its Microsoft 365 and information-security rules.
- **Everyday reporting, not deep analysis.** Excel is used for summaries and figure checks. Trend analysis, exceptions and dashboards belong to the separate program, AI-Powered HR Data Analysis & Decision Intelligence.
- **A prototype, not a deployed system.** Participants leave with tested prompt sets and an assistant prototype. Workflow automation with Power Automate and Copilot Studio is not part of this program.
- **Recruitment at a high level only.** It is covered in one Day 2 block.
