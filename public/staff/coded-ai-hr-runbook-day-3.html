A request on a timer: a scheduled prompt

No file needed.
▶Say“You already have good requests in your Prompt Bank. A scheduled prompt runs one of them for you, at the time you choose, so the answer is waiting when you arrive.”
This demo must proveA tested request can run on its own at a set time. It writes for you only; it does not send, route or approve.
Script (stage directions in brackets)
  1. (Paste the weekly request and run it once.) Before I schedule anything, I check the answer is worth getting every week.
  2. (Hover over the request. Select Schedule this prompt.) Weekly, Sunday, 8:00. Save.
  3. (Open Settings, then Scheduled prompts.) Here I can edit, pause or delete it. Up to ten per person.
  4. It runs for me only, from what I can already see. On Sunday the answer is waiting in my Copilot chats, and I still check it.
Discuss with the room
  • Which request from your Prompt Bank would you schedule, and when? Listen for: A weekly summary of requests, a Monday reminder draft, a monthly policy update check.
  • Can a scheduled prompt approve a training request? Listen for: No. It only writes an answer for you. Moving work between people needs a flow: you see that before lunch.
Click steps
  1. Trainer: In Copilot Chat on your work account, paste the request below and run it once. Read the answer: is it worth getting every week?
  2. Trainer: Hover over your request and select Schedule this prompt. Set it to every week, sunday at 8:00. Save.
  3. Trainer: Open Settings, then Scheduled prompts. Show where to edit, pause or delete it.
  4. Trainer: Say: it runs for me only, from what I can already see. The answer waits in my Copilot chats, and I still check it.
Prompts: paste in this order
A weekly request to scheduleLook at my email and Teams messages from the last 7 days about training requests and vendor quotations. Make a table: who asked or who sent it, the course, the date, and what is still waiting for my reply. Then list my three most urgent actions. Leave out anything about pay, health or a named person's performance.
What you should seeOne run now: a table of training requests and quotations with what is waiting, and three actions, with nothing about pay or health. Then the same request saved in Scheduled prompts, every week, sunday at 8:00.
!If it failsNo Schedule this prompt option: the account has no Microsoft 365 Copilot license, or the admin turned it off. Show the steps on the slide and the Scheduled prompts list on the trainer laptop. The result is on the backup results page.

10Demo 3.5 · Power Automate · recorded demoIn the slide

A training request approval (recorded flow)

Files: power-automate-demo-1-training-request.mp4, mutla-training-request-log.xlsx
▶Say“A flow starts on its own and does the same steps every time. When a decision is needed, it stops and waits for a person.”
This demo must proveA flow starts on its own, does the same steps every time, and stops to wait when a person must decide.
Script (stage directions in brackets)
  1. (Play. Pause on the first card.) Four parts, the same in every flow: trigger, actions, connectors, output.
  2. (Pause on 1 · TRIGGER.) What starts it: a new answer in the training request form. Nobody presses a button.
  3. (Pause on Start and wait for an approval.) The flow stops here, for hours or days if needed, until a person decides.
  4. (Pause on the condition.) Approve takes one path. Reject takes the other.
  5. (Play to RESULT.) The approval took a minute and a half. Every other step took seconds. (Play to the end.) A row in Excel that nobody typed, and an email to the employee.
Discuss with the room
  • Which step did a person do? Listen for: The approval. Everything else is the same every time.
  • Where does a request like this wait in your unit today? Listen for: Signatures, email, printing and scanning.
Click steps
  1. Trainer: Click play on the video (no sound). Pause on each step card and name the part: trigger, action, connector, output.
  2. Trainer: Pause after Start and wait for an approval. Ask the room which step a person does.
  3. Trainer: Play to the end: the run took seconds except the approval. Point to the new Excel row and the email.
What you should seeThe steps: trigger: When a new response is submitted; action: Get response details; action: Start and wait for an approval; action: Add a row into a table; condition: Outcome is equal to Approve; action: Send an email (V2). One form response becomes one approval, one row in the log and one email. The approver decides; the flow only carries the request.
!If it failsIf the video does not play, open it from the Day 3 page (sample files) or the USB stick. If that fails too, walk through the four parts on the slide and the step table in the backup. The result is on the backup results page.
