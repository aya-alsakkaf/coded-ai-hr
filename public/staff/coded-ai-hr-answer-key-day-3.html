Schedule one of your requests

No file
Hint (the participant pages show this)Pick something you do every week: a summary, a reminder, a list of what is still waiting. Daily is often too much.
Answer

A good result

  • The request ran once and the answer was worth reading again.
  • Weekly or monthly, not daily, unless the person truly needs it every day.
  • The request leaves out pay, health and named people's performance.
  • It shows in Settings, then Scheduled prompts, and the person knows how to pause it.

Task 3.7

A map of one HR process, with problems and human decisions marked.
Task 3.7 · 20 min · on your own

Map your own HR process

mutla-process-stories.docx, mutla-process-map-template.docx
Hint (the participant pages show this)Start where the request starts, and stop when the requester has the result.
Answer

What each story hides

An HR letter request

  • Delay: the letter waits on the supervisor's desk.
  • Duplicated effort: each letter retyped from an old one.
  • Unclear ownership: nobody records issue, three people search.
  • Repetitive step: writing requests on notes; calling to collect.

A remote-site allowance claim

  • Delay: the site supervisor replies after a week; claims after the 20th wait a month.
  • Duplicated effort: typed into a spreadsheet, the payroll form and the payroll system.
  • Unclear ownership: each officer answers status questions from their own notes.
  • Person decides: the supervisor confirms days; the Compensation Supervisor approves.

A request for a new position

  • Delay: the memo waits when someone is on leave.
  • Duplicated effort: three emails to collect the facts; two files to check.
  • Unclear ownership: nobody knows the latest version or where the request is.
  • Person decides: Supervisor, Finance and the division head approve.

A job re-evaluation request

  • Delay: a file that misses the agenda waits a month.
  • Duplicated effort: printing copies; typing the result twice.
  • Unclear ownership: nobody tells the unit the result first.
  • Repetitive step: asking units again for missing documents. Person decides: the committee.

Task 3.8

An improved workflow card for your process.
Task 3.8 · 25 min · on your own

Optional: improve your map

mutla-workflow-card-template.docx
Hint (the participant pages show this)Start with the steps you marked as a problem. Which can disappear, and which can Copilot or an agent help with?
Answer

A strong card has

  • Every step marked Person, Copilot or Agent, with one owner.
  • Every approval and decision with a person.
  • At least two problems from the map removed.
  • No real names or real data.

The example card

Card lineExample
Process and trackA training request · Learning and Development
Trigger: what starts itThe employee sends the standard training request form by email.
Inputs: what the request must containEmployee code, course name, course start date, reason, line manager's email. No names in free text.
Steps: each one marked Person, Copilot or Agent1 Check the training policy (Agent) · 2 Send the standard form (Person) · 3 Line manager approves by reply (Person) · 4 L&D approves (Person) · 5 Send the result with a template drafted once (Copilot) · 6 Log the request once (Person)
Decisions: which steps a person decidesThe line manager and L&D approve. Copilot and the agent decide nothing.
Owners: one owner for every stepEmployee: the request. Line manager: step 3. L&D: step 4. Training Coordinator: steps 5 and 6, and the reminders.
Outputs: what the requester gets, and what is loggedThe employee gets an email with the decision. One row in the training tracker.
Removed: the delays, duplicates and repeated steps you took outNo printing or scanning, no typing twice, and a reminder after 2 working days instead of nobody chasing.
One piece you built: a Copilot request or agent instructionsAgent instructions for a Training Policy Helper: it answers from the training policy and refers personal cases to L&D.
