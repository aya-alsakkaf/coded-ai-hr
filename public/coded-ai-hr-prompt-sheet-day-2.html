Agenda, brief and discussion points

Watch it in the slide. The trainer runs it with the room.
Prepare the meeting
You are a Personnel officer at Mutla Oil Company. We meet for 30 minutes on Monday to plan the orientation day for new joiners. Personnel, IT and L&D attend. Prepare an agenda, a one-paragraph background brief and three discussion points. The facts: 18 new joiners start on Sunday 6 December 2026. The orientation day runs from 8:00 to 13:00 in Training Room 2, head office. Personnel and Administration prepares the ID cards; IT prepares 18 laptop accounts. L&D runs the welcome session and the safety briefing. Open question: can the Medical Services check be done on the same day? Use only these facts. Give each agenda item an owner and minutes that add up to 30. Turn each open question into a discussion point. Short and practical. An agenda table (item, owner, minutes), then the brief, then the discussion points. Start with: DRAFT · Reviewed by: ___
What to look forDo the minutes add up to 30?
FilesNo file needed.
TakeawayAgenda, brief, discussion points: from approved facts only.

Block 4 · Forms: training needs

Demo 2.7Copilot ChatWatch, then try

From a vague ask to requirements

Watch it in the slide. The trainer runs it with the room.
A weak start
Create a training needs survey for our employees.
Requirements first
You are a Learning and Development specialist at Mutla Oil Company. L&D builds the 2027 training plan from a training needs survey that 52 line managers answer in December. Last year's survey was a Word form, and the answers were hard to count. Before writing any question, list the decisions L&D must make from this survey and the information each decision needs. Then suggest one question for each piece of information. Use only the attached brief, mutla-training-needs-brief-2027.docx. At most 10 questions. Every question serves one decision. One idea per question. Use choices wherever the brief gives a list. Never ask for an employee's name, civil ID, performance rating, salary or health. A table: decision, information needed, question, question type (choice, number or text), required (yes or no).
What to look forFor every question, ask: which decision does it serve?
Files
mutla-training-needs-brief-2027.docx
TakeawayDecisions first. Then the information. Then the questions.
Task 2.7Copilot ChatYour turn · 12 min

Brainstorm the requirements

  1. Choose one. A: the company's 2027 training needs survey, from mutla-training-needs-brief-2027.docx. B: a training needs form for your own unit or department.
  2. Write your own checklist request: the decisions first, then the information each decision needs, then one question for each. A: attach the brief. B: write three or four facts about your unit as your Data.
  3. Test every question three ways: Which decision does it serve? Can a manager answer it in under a minute? Does it ask for anyone's personal data? Delete what fails.
  4. Keep eight questions or fewer. Write your question plan: question, type (choice, number or text), the choices, required or not.
You hand in

A question plan of eight questions or fewer: question, type, choices, required.

Finished early? Extra task
  • Ask Copilot: which of my questions could a manager misread? Rewrite the two weakest.
What to look forStart from the five decisions in the brief. Which question does each one need?
Files
mutla-training-needs-brief-2027.docx
TakeawayEvery question serves a decision. Nothing asks about one person.
